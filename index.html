02 — Aerial AI

Twenty-eight answers out of one flight

The flight is cheap; reading it is the expensive part. Our engine takes the footage and returns counts, violations, incidents and change — automatically, with a dated report at the end. A thirty-minute recording is processed in about twenty minutes on a single workstation.

The catalogue beside this text is real work, not a wish list: it was specified with a Gulf customer and built against measured acceptance criteria — people counting within ±10%, intrusion recall above 90%, fire above 85%, shoreline position within three metres.

  • New analyses are added for a market without rebuilding the platform

03 — Digital twin

A twin of the site you can actually operate

We build a three-dimensional twin of the facility — the buildings, the equipment, the vehicles, the cameras and the sensors, each one standing where it really stands. It is not a rendering for a brochure. Every object in the model carries its own live state, and the ones that can be operated are operated from inside it.

The operator stops reading a list of device codes and starts looking at the site. A warning is not a row in a table any more; it is a pump on the second floor, glowing, with its history and its camera one click away.

This is the product that won us Dubai. One of the largest retail developments in the world — 303,000 m² — selected us to build it, and the screenshot beside this text is that system. Security, operations and the aerial layer all hang off the same twin.

08 — New

GNSS interference and mission integrity

Jamming and spoofing in the Gulf stopped being a rare event. In March 2025 the IMO, ICAO and ITU issued a joint warning about satellite navigation interference affecting aviation, shipping and critical services, and Arabian Gulf reporting through 2026 describes hundreds of vessels affected in incidents lasting hours.

For a drone operator this is a commercial problem, not a political one: the mission is flown, the customer is invoiced, and nobody can prove the position data was sound. We turn that into a monitored, reportable service.

  • Buyers: ports, oil and gas, airports, critical facilities, security integrators
  • Sells as a monthly fee per site, not a one-off project
  • We are software and passive sensing only — licensed RF work stays with the local partner

09 — New

Remote site connectivity cockpit

A drone dock on a remote site is worthless the moment the link drops, and today those links are a mixture of satellite terminals, LTE and whatever else the site has. Nobody owns the single picture. We build it: every site, every link, one screen, with alerts before the operation notices.

It reads the customer's own terminals with their permission — no third-party traffic is touched, and no spectrum licence is needed to run it.

  • Buyers: energy, ports and marine, mining, remote facilities, emergency services

  • Natural attachment to any drone-in-a-box deployment you sell

10 — The whole site

Cameras, drones, ground vehicles and people — one control centre

Most facilities buy their security in pieces: a camera contract, a guard contract, and lately a drone. Each one has its own screen and its own supplier, and when something happens three people look at three systems and phone each other.

We close that loop. Everything on the site reports into one map and one log; the system classifies what it sees, ranks it, and sends the nearest unit — the camera that already has the angle, the drone from its dock, the ground robot, or the guard who is closest. The operator approves and can override at any point, and every step is written into a dated record that survives an audit.

  • Buyers: industrial sites, ports, logistics yards, campuses, developments, critical facilities
  • Replaces nothing the customer already bought — it sits on top and connects it
  • Detection, dispatch and evidence only — nothing armed, nothing weaponised
